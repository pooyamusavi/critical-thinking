Critical Thinking vs. Constructive Thinking Expanding How We Know Beyond Pure Formalism

Traditional Critical Thinking relies heavily on the scientific paradigm—valuing only what can be formalized, operationalized, and proven through detached logic. Constructive Thinking (Barbara Thayer-Bacon) retains reason while expanding our epistemology to embrace embodied tacit knowing (Polanyi), fully conscious intuition (Noddings & Shore), emotion, imagination, and relational context.

Primary Text Spotlight • Noddings & Shore (1984, p. 54)

The Spectrum of Knowing: Why Intuition ≠ Tacit Knowing

Both Michael Polanyi’s Tacit Knowing and Noddings & Shore’s Intuition defy the strict scientific paradigm because neither can be formalized into rigid rules. However, they diverge sharply on one vital dimension: Conscious Awareness.

Interactive Primary Excerpt — Click any highlighted phrase or select a Mental Mode below Source: Noddings & Shore (1984, p. 54), cited in Thayer-Bacon
“Intuition, as Noddings and Shore describe, shares the qualities that Polanyi mentioned with tacit knowing, in that it is unanalyzable and not something that can be formalized and operationalized. It does not fit the scientific paradigm of thought. And yet, unlike Polanyi, intuition is something that we are conscious of. ‘No mental mode could be more fully conscious than the intuitive mode’ (1984, p. 54).”
